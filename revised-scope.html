Revised scope · Wednesday 7 October

You Are Not Buying Leads. You Are Buying Back Four Months You Already Pay For.

Lansing Mechanical LLC · revised after our call of 6 October · nothing on this page is priced
What changed, and why

On our call I said I would go back and alter the scope and the price, because I did not want to charge you for something you do not need. This is that.

Two things came out of the call that changed it:

  • Your problem is not that summer is quiet. Summer is at capacity. Your problem is October through January.
  • You have been sold leads before and it did not work. So the thing I am proposing is not leads.
Your numbers, as you gave them to me
  What you said
Summer About 10 calls a day, roughly 50 service calls a week
October to January "We may only have one or two" a day
What you could handle in winter "Easily five to 10 a day during that time"
Installs Two a week, and consistent all year
Average ticket About $500
The team Tyler, Jimmy, Kane and Blake, plus your husband, plus you
Trading 16 years. Trane and Mitsubishi. Revenue consistent rather than growing
The thing I did not say well enough on the call

Your payroll does not drop in October. Four technicians and your husband are paid through October, November, December and January while the work falls from ten calls a day to one or two.

So a winter service call is worth considerably more to you than a summer one. In summer you are at capacity, and more demand simply queues. In winter the capacity is already bought, already paid for, and sitting idle.

On your own figures, thirteen weeks of November to January at twenty calls a week instead of about seven — still well inside the five to ten a day you told me you can handle — is in the region of 170 extra service calls. At your $500 average ticket that is roughly $85,000 of revenue that does not currently exist.

⚠ That is multiplication of your numbers to show the size of the gap. It is not a forecast, a projection or a promise, and I am not going to dress it up as one. I do not know your conversion rate, your costs or your margins, and anyone who puts a predicted figure in front of you after one call is making it up.

You asked me how I guarantee the leads are not bogus. Here is my honest answer.

I am not going to guarantee that. It is the same sentence the last company said to you, and you already know what it was worth.

What I will do instead is make it so you do not have to trust me.

Here is why the last one failed, mechanically rather than as a complaint. You paid per lead — a name and a phone number. That company got paid whether the person wanted anything or not, which means their incentive was volume and yours was revenue. Those two things were never pointed in the same direction.

What I am proposing is a different unit. An enquiry that arrives with the job type, the timeframe and the zip code attached, through a form on your own website, recorded with its source. And:

  • You will be able to see every enquiry, where it came from, and whether it was junk — within about two weeks, because right now your site has no form and no tracking at all, so you can see nothing.
  • If the enquiries are poor, you will know before you have spent much, and so will I.
  • You already told me you value the automatic reply, because "at least somebody's going to get back to them, so they don't feel like they're not being answered." That is part of this, and it answers for you at the times you cannot.
The order I would do it in
  • First, so that anything after it is measurable. A proper enquiry form on every page, the phone number tappable everywhere, analytics and conversion tracking installed, and the placeholder text taken off your homepage. Nothing can be judged until this exists.
  • Second, the pages that do the selling. Your reviews are on Google and not on your site. Your licence, your warranty and the fact that you are a Trane and Mitsubishi dealer are not stated where a buyer looks. And your own words on the call — "we're not like Joe and his pickup truck" — are not anywhere on the website at all. They should be.
  • Third, and only then, the slow season. Advertising aimed at October through January, switched on with tracking already in place, so that from the first week you can see what it costs and what it books.

⚠ Why the order and not all at once: if the advertising goes first, you end up in exactly the position you were in before — money leaving, no way to tell what it bought. I would rather you be able to fire me in week three on evidence than keep me on faith.

What you should judge this on

Booked jobs per month, November to January, against last winter.

⚠ You cannot currently make that comparison, because nothing has ever recorded it. That is not a criticism; it is the single best reason to put the measurement in first.

⭐ And one thing I will not ask you to judge it on: how the website looks. You said it yourself and I agree with you. Simple scales. Fancy fails.

What this page deliberately does not contain
  • No price. We will go through the revised scope and the number together on the call, and I would rather you heard it from me with the reasoning than read it off a page.
  • No guarantee, for the reason above.
  • No predicted number of leads, cost per lead, or ranking. You have no baseline, so any figure I gave you today would be invented.
